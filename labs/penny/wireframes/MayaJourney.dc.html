Journey · early career

Maya, 26: "Help me stop feeling behind without becoming a finance person."

14 months into her first job
The numbers

Data analyst, 72k. Take-home 4,300 a month. Rent 1,650, essentials 3,000. Checking 3,400. Savings 1,200 at 0.5%. Student loans 28,400 at 5.5%, minimum 310. 401(k): eligible, not enrolled. Employer matches 100% up to 4%. Free cash about 420 a month, currently unallocated.

Money story

Parents did not talk about money. What she knows came from short videos and one Reddit thread. She knows she "should invest" and is afraid of locking money away and of picking wrong. The benefits enrollment felt like a tax form, so she closed it.

Anxieties

"What if I need the money and it is locked up?"
"What if I pick the wrong fund?"
"Am I already behind everyone?"
Runway is 0.4 months. She does not know that number, but she feels it.

Tried and abandoned

A budgeting app (three weeks, too much categorizing). A savings challenge (two months). Opening the benefits portal at 11pm (gave up at fund selection).

Trust threshold and success

Trusts it when the math is in her numbers, when she can undo, and when it never makes her feel judged. Success in 12 months: enrolled at the full match, fund automated, runway visibly climbing toward 3 months by month 34.

Prepares two approval cards with the exact paycheck effect (190 less take-home), the effective date, and the undo. On approval, executes through the plan provider and the bank. Logs both. Schedules a check that it landed.

Risk and guardrail
Nagging kills trust. One moment at a time; back off after two ignores; quiet hours she controls.
A wrong extraction is worse than none. Every fact shows its source; she can correct it in place; nothing inferred is hidden.
No number without a calculator. Assumptions editable. No fund or security recommendations.
Overconfidence. Confidence stated; "what would change my mind" listed; fund questions escalate to a licensed advisor.
Nothing moves without a confirm. The plan's own default investment applies; Penny picks nothing. Reversible.
